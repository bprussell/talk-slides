# Talk notes: framing decisions

## Subject
How AI is changing the work within jobs, using Brad's own software engineering role (2023 to 2026) as the example. The posting lag is a supporting point, not the thesis.

## Supporting point: postings lag
Job postings can lag what employers actually hire for. The gap is structural, not careless.

## Why postings lag (Brad's framing, 10/26)
1. **Some qualities can't be screened by writing them down.** We hire for judgment, but "must have good judgment" is useless in a posting because nearly everyone believes they have it. Judgment gets assessed in the interview (e.g., "give me a judgment call you made"), not advertised.
2. **The people who maintain postings have other jobs.** Expectations shift faster than anyone has time to rewrite the JD. Our current posting isn't fully up to date for exactly this reason.

Takeaway: the postings you see in the field may not reflect employers' current expectations.

## Don't say
- Avoid absolutes and hot takes ("always", "never", "everyone"). Brad wants a measured tone.
- Don't frame the 2026 JD's AI bullets as a failure or "bolted on." The posting isn't wrong; it's a lagging, lossy signal.

## Tie-ins to the workshop
- For the room: the gap between your posting and your real job is itself data.
- For the university signal: curriculum built from postings alone can trail what employers are hiring for. Talk to hiring managers too.
